The Vessel of Beginnings
Before the ceremony begins, an egg is prepared — a vessel holding everything we are about to use. An egg is one of the oldest symbols of renewal, protection, and the unbroken potential of what is yet to come. It is cracked open only when we are ready to begin.
  • The evil eye necklace — to be placed around Lena's neck as the final act of protection
  • A length of red thread — to be tied at her wrist, knotted seven times with seven wishes
  • A piece of paper bearing the letter L — for Lena, and for the whole family she carries with her: Lena, Jim, Maya, and Dom
  • A small sprig of sage — a reminder that the clearing has already begun
When to open it The egg is brought out at the start of the rites and placed in the centre of the circle, still whole. It is cracked open by Lena herself at the moment the rites begin — a deliberate breaking of the old and the beginning of the new. The items inside are laid out in front of her, ready to be used in turn as the ceremony unfolds.

The Rites · In Order
Cast out the bad first.
Then call in the good.
The candles come after — flames lit into a room already cleared.
Banishing
🌿 Sage Smudging
The Officiant opens the egg and invites Lena to crack it. The items inside are laid out in the centre of the circle. Then the Sage Carrier lights the bundle and leads everyone through each room — corners included — while the Officiant sets the intention aloud: "We are clearing this home of everything that does not serve Lena." The group responds: "We are clearing this home."
Windows open before you begin. Move clockwise. Fan smoke into corners. Cover the smoke alarm in advance.
Sealing
🧂 Salt Solution Perimeter
The Salt Bearer leads everyone around the home — along the outside if possible, or along interior windowsills and doorways — sprinkling salt solution as they go. When the circuit is complete, the Threshold Voice says: "Nothing unwelcome crosses this threshold." The circle echoes: "Nothing unwelcome."
Salt is one of the oldest known protections. It doesn't care what you believe.
Release
🔥 Writing & Burning
Two rounds. Papers are private — what is written stays between the writer and the flame. Take as many slips as you need.
Round One · For Lena & Her Family
The Officiant says: "Write what you want gone for Lena and her family — a worry, a weight, a run of bad luck. Write as many as you like." When everyone is ready, each person places their papers in the bowl one by one. The Fire Keeper lights each one. As each burns, everyone says together: "Gone. Done. Over."
Round Two · For All of Us
The Officiant says: "Now write anything any of us would like to leave behind today — for yourself, for someone else in this circle, for all of us. This bowl holds whatever we need it to." Same process: papers placed, lit, burned. "Gone. Done. Over." The Officiant closes: "It's done. The room is clear. Now we bring in the light."
Do this near an open window. The Fire Keeper keeps a damp cloth nearby.
Calling In
🕯️ Three Candles & Spoken Intention
Three candles are lit in sequence. The Candle — Lena bearer lights the first: "This flame is for Lena." The Candle — Family bearer lights the second: "This flame is for Lena, Jim, Maya, and Dom." The Candle — Circle bearer lights the third: "And this one is for all of us."

Then the Officiant invites everyone to speak a wish or blessing aloud — for Lena, for the family, or for the group. Anyone who would rather hold their wish in silence is equally welcome to do so. Both land the same.
Go around the circle. A moment of quiet from someone is not opting out — it is a different form of the same act.
Binding
🧵 Red Thread
The Thread Tier wraps the red thread around Lena's wrist and ties it seven times. As each knot is made, someone from the circle speaks a wish — for Lena, for Jim, for Maya, for Dom, and for the life they are building together. The thread is worn until it falls away on its own. When it does, the wishes are released into the world.
The last knot: "The protection you wear, you carry home to all of them."
Protection
🧿 The Evil Eye
The Necklace Placer puts the evil eye necklace around Lena's neck as the final act of the ceremony. It has already known care and intention. Now it holds hers.
Everyone says together: "May every eye that wishes you harm look away."
Setting the Scene
Music
Something wordless and warm — Ólafur Arnalds, Nils Frahm, or search "ritual ambient" or "sacred feminine" playlists. No lyrics during the rites.
Lighting
Candles, fairy lights, dimmed overheads. The space should feel like something is happening here.
Formation
Sit in a circle for the rites — floor is ideal, with cushions and throws. Lena can sit in the centre or within the circle; wherever feels right.
What to Bring
For the Ceremony
Sage bundle · Salt solution in a small bottle · Red thread · Three candles + long matches · Plenty of paper scraps + pens · A metal bowl for burning · The evil eye necklace · The egg
For the Hosting
Disposable plates & cups · Napkins · A bin bag · Food and drink. Leave nothing behind that Lena has to deal with afterwards.
